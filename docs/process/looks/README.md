# Keeper office look options

Open `index.html` in a modern browser with WebGL and internet access. Select A/B/C with the buttons or keys 1/2/3. Each selection restarts the same 25-second scripted demo. These are visual options, not a live policy engine or a final product view. Reduced motion shows the BLOCK moment at 16 seconds without animation. Room sensitivities are illustrative: Clients is client-confidential; Finance and Admin are regulated; Inbox and Notes are internal; Marketing is public.

**A · Low-poly studio.** Warm cream surroundings, low partitions, wooden desks, small plants and rounded faceted characters make the office feel approachable. Soft daylight and toy-like proportions suit a founder-facing morning briefing; the furniture adds visual detail that competes slightly with folder labels on narrow screens.

**B · Match night.** A dark striped pitch, floodlight towers, strong directional shadows, pale player figures and a stadium-style scoreboard emphasize the Keeper and outgoing shots. It gives the BLOCK and HOLD moments more drama, while the dark environment makes the office metaphor less literal. Captions and labels retain high contrast.

**C · Blueprint.** A pale grid, thin colored room boundaries, simplified desks and geometric agents put folder names and decisions first. It is the clearest direction for explaining scope and reviewing activity; the intentionally restrained geometry gives it less character than the studio or match-night options.

The room tint communicates sensitivity, while written labels provide the same information without relying on color. All geometry, including the goal net and ball, is generated in code. The only external dependency is the exact three.js 0.160.0 module in the import map. There are no external models, textures, fonts or orbit controls. At phone widths, the scene fits the available canvas and captions wrap below it; at video widths, room labels increase in size.
